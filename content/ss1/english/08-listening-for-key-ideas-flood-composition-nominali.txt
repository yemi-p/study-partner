Exclamation Mark (!) and the Question Mark (?)

Exclamation Mark (!)

An exclamation has the single sign!
It is used to show emotions e.g. anger, delight, repulsion, disappointment, surprise, admiration.

Examples

Stop it! You fool! (anger)

Oh! Hug me! (delight)

Thate it! (repulsion)

I should have known it! (disappointment)
You made it! (surprise)

How beautiful! (admiration)

No matter the degree of emotion expressed, the exclamation mark remains single '!' It is incorrect to use
double !!, triple !!! or more signs. Ifused within a quotation mark, do not follow it with another punctuation
mark. It is incorrect to write: 'Go away!,' the teacher bellowed. However, it is correct to write: 'Go away!"
the teacher bellowed.

The Origin of Silk

In 1973, a Japanese sericulturist arrived in Malawi with a batch of 40,000 silkworm eggs. They were taken
to the Bvumbwe Agricultural Research Station in Thyolo District. In this station, work is being done to
determine favourable silkworm rearing conditions and areas where mulberry trees, whose leaves the
worms feed on, could grow well. According to researchers, the silkworms which eventually develop into
cocoons from which raw silk is produced do well in areas with warm climatic conditions.

Silk is one of the strongest of fibres. In fact, for thousands of years, silk fabrics have been regarded as
the most beautiful and durable materials woven by man. Many people call silk the 'cloth of kings and
queens’.

The weaving of silk originated in China. An old Chinese book believed to be written by Confucius, tells
us that the wife of Emperor Huang-ti was the first person to make fabrics of silk. Around 2640 B.C.,
Emperor Huang-ti asked his wife Hsi Ling-Shih to study the worms that were destroying the mulberry trees
in his garden. The Empress took some of the cocoons into the palace to see what they were made of. She
dropped one of the cocoons into a bowl of boiling water and was amazed to see a cobweb-like tangle
separate itself from the cocoon. She picked up the gauzy mass and found that one of the threads could be
unwound almost without end from the cocoon. Hsi Ling-Shih had discovered silk. She was delighted with
the discovery and even wove a ceremonial robe for the Emperor out of the cocoon threads. After that, the
officials in the Emperor's court wore brightly dyed silk robes on important occasions.

People in other countries regarded the new fibres as something rare and beautiful. A few traders went
to China to learn about making cloth from silk, but the Chinese kept their silkworms a closely guarded
secret.

(Source: JME 1984 Use of English)

Descriptive Essay (1)

A descriptive essay is a composition in which the writer paints a vivid picture of what is known to him to his
readers, so that they can visualize with their inner eyes what is being described. Therefore, a descriptive
essay must be stuffed with details, well-chosen language and correctly punctuated sentences. The more the
detail that is added, the more information the reader receives.

Consider this description

As the opulent, superfluously dressed young man quietly pulled up, a skeletal body wrapped in dirty, smelly,
tattered clothes, staggered up on his mosquito legs. He twisted his vulture - like neck towards the parked
poshy light green V-boot Mercedez Benz saloon car and fixed a hollow deeply set eyes on its owner.
Thrusting out his weak, weather-beaten right hand, he weakly mumbled some words in ellipses.

Without being told who is being described, the picture being painted tells you immediately. Discuss this
with your teacher.

For you to describe effectively, you must use a number of devices.

Choice of Words

You must have a rich vocabulary and use it effectively. You acquire this through reading widely. For
example, you can use several words to describe sounds produced by a person, such as_ laughed,
whispered, chuckled, giggled, roared, muttered, bellowed, thundered, whimpered, gasped, drawled,
moaned, chanted, squealed, tittered, stammered, chimed, mumbled, shrilled, etc (look up those you
do not know their meanings in your dictionary).

Comparison and Contrast

Compare and contrast an object/a situation with the one your reader is familiar with e.g. If you write
to your European pen-pal, you can compare the Harmattan season with the Autumn which he is
familiar with.

Tenses

Use tenses as suitable. Use present tense for every day occurrences and past tense for events that had
happened. Most often you use a mixture of the two as you find you are both narrating and describing.

Organizing a Descriptive Essay

Decide on the aspects you want to describe and describe them sequentially.

Examples

1. If you are describing a person, include the following aspects in that order: physical appearance,
educational background, character, achievements and finally, your evaluation of him/her.

2. If you are describing an event that happened in a day, describe what happened in the morning first,
then afternoon and finally night before evaluating the day.

3. If you are describing a place, include some of the following aspects: appearance, location, size, type,

tone, its special qualities, activities associated with it and your final reaction to the place.

There is no hard and fast rule about where to begin. You may start from the more general and move to
the particular, or vice versa. That is, you may start by describing the general appearance of the place,
or start by describing the individual rooms it contains. The choice is yours.

However, do not bring in too many aspects into your essay, as you may not have enough space to
describe all fully well and effectively.
