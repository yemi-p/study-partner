In section 1.2 of the last unit, we saw that each sentence could be divided into three parts. Now, we
are going to look more closely at the first and third parts of each sentence. Here they are:

(i) (a) Ade (b) _ the teacher.

(ii) (a) Ngozi (b) _ the visitor.

(iii) (a) Lawal (b) the book.

(iv) (a) I (b) — the book.

(v) (a) Dapo (b) the examination.

Notice that all the (a) words above are names of persons. The person in each case does
something, or has done something. The (b) words refer to persons or things. So the (a) and (b)
words do have something in common. In fact, you can change them round in sentences (i) and (ii)
of Unit I section 1.2 so that you have:

(i) The teacher admires Ade; Ade admires the teacher.

(ii) The visitor greeted Ngozi; Ngozi greeted the visitor.

We therefore say that all the words in (i) to (v) in section 1.0 above belong to the same part of speech
or form class because so far as the meaning will allow, one of them can be used to replace another in
asentence. They are all nouns.

Now, let us compare the (a) words with the (b) words. The (a) words stand alone Ade, Ngozi, Lawal,
I, Dapo. These are words which, with the exception of J, are the names of people. We shall say more

about J later. These names of people are proper nouns.

On the other hand, the (b) words are in two parts, as follows:

(i) the /teacher

(ii) the /visitor

(iti) & (iv) the /book

(v) the /examination

Notice that in each case although the word in the second part does refer to a person or thing the word
is not the name of a person. Because of this, the word has to be preceded by the definite article
'the'. These words are known as common nouns.

So, words which are names of persons are not preceded by the definite article, but words which
merely refer to persons, and those which refer to things, are usually preceded by the definite article.

Names of villages, towns and countries are also treated like the names of persons. So we have:
(i) Ibadan is bigger than Jos.

(ii) Nigeria is bigger than Ghana.
(ili) Ikeja is in Lagos State.
(iv) Africa is not too far from Europe.

This is generally the case, but there are a few exceptions such as The Ivory Coast in West Africa and
The Hague in Europe.

Introduction

A narrative essay is a writing in which a story is told. Everybody at one time or the other, tells a story. For
example, if you return from school and either of your parents asks you what you did at school that day, what
you tell him or her is a narrative.

‘Oh, Dad! It was not an easy day for me. Almost everything went wrong. You know I left home late, as I
had to wash Uncle Joe's car, as well as yours before I left.

T arrived at school at 8.05 a.m. and was caught for late-coming. I was made to pick litter round the
school during the first period. Unfortunately, my class had a Mathematics test then and you know that is my
best subject. I missed that test, which will definitely lower my Continuous Assessment Score!

If your narration went that way, you would have written a narrative essay!

The introductory paragraph \eads the writer's father into the type of day he had. The next three middle
paragraphs serving as the body of the narration, narrate the events of the day that support the claim in the
introductory paragraph, ofa bad day.

The concluding paragraph sums up the writer's general feelings about the day.

Note the following:

1. The events of the day narrated are past, so past tense of verbs is used. You should use the same tense,

when you write your own narration.

2. The events are narrated sequentially from the start of the school day to the close of it. Your own

narration must be sequential.

3. | The introductory paragraph arouses the writer's father's curiosity to know why and how the day was
bad. Your own introduction must be interesting enough to arouse the same feeling of curiosity in

your listeners/readers.

4. The Body - Events of the day were discussed, paragraph by paragraph. However, two or more events

that are brief, can be merged in a paragraph. Point out the events discussed in each paragraph. The

body of your own narration must follow a similar pattern.

5. The concluding paragraph is brief and gives the writer's final feelings about the day. Your own
conclusion must be brief and satisfying.

Full stop (.): (Written as two separate words)

The full stop, signified by a dot, is a very important punctuation mark. When we speak, we pause from time
to time for breath and to let our listeners know that we have come to the end of one idea and are about to
begin another. Similarly, when we write, we should indicate these pauses. This enables our readers to
follow point by point, the ideas we are putting across.

Read the following:
They left for the market that afternoon as they were near the market they heard a shout it sounded near they
looked back to their delight it had come froma friend they were going to see at the market.

14

The passage contains five sentences which are muddled up, owing to lack of full stops. Put full stops at
the correct places, without looking at the one done below. If you do it correctly, you will have this:

They left for the market that afternoon. As they were near the market, they heard a shout. It sounded
near. They looked back. To their delight, it had come from a friend they were going to see at the market.

You will notice that the full stop appeared where there are Jong pauses. They have separated one idea
from another. In other words, a full stop ends a sentence. Note too, that wherever a full stop appears, the
word following it begins with a capital letter.

Other Uses of Full Stops
1. It is used to indicate that a word has been shortened. Examine these:

3. Itis used at the end of the date when written with the address e.g. 11th April, 1986.

Words Associated with Agriculture

Farming is a universal occupation. In Nigeria today, almost everyone is a farmer. If you plant vegetables
in your kitchen-garden for home use you are definitely a farmer!

The basic farming implements are the hoe and the cutlass. The cutlass is used to clear the farmland
and also to get rid of weeds after the crops have been planted. The hoe is used for weeding but more
importantly, it is used to make heaps for planting root-crops like yam, potato, cassava and cocoyam.

These are mostly planted in the forest zone of the south and the middle belt area of the country. The hoe
is also used to make ridges for crops like groundnuts, tomatoes, okro and vegetables.

In the grasslands of the middle belt and the north, mechanised farming is more commonly practised.
Instead of hoes and cutlasses, tractors are used to clear large expanse of land. Grains or cereals such as
rice, millet, sorghum, wheat and maize are commonly planted.

The farmer often applies manure (animal-dung, poultry droppings and vegetable cuttings). These
enrich the soil and so the farmer is assured of a good yield or a bumper harvest.

With irrigation it is now possible to have two planting seasons instead of planting only during the
rainy season. Irrigation has also turned the arid land of the far-north into suitable farmland.

Whatever a farmer and his family cannot immediately consume or sell off is preserved. Tubers are
often stored in airy barns which are placed above the ground. These tubers may also be dusted with
chemicals which are harmless to humans before putting them on flat tops off the ground.

The traditional method of preserving grains is to hang them up by the fireplace. They can also be kept
in wicker-baskets and woven-grass barns. Nowadays, pesticides are applied to these grains before
storage. They can be stored in sacks, or air-tight containers. In the case of very large yields, they are kept in
grain silos above or under the ground.

2.2

Compare a drink and a bottle with two glasses and many boys. A drink means one drink, and a
bottle, one bottle. So, the indefinite article refers to only one object, in contrast to two objects as in
two glasses, and more than two, as in many boys. We say that drink, bottle, boy and glass are
countable nouns.

Because we cannot count ‘juice’, we say some fruit juice. We therefore say that juice is a non-
countable noun. Other examples of non-countable nouns are water (some water), beer (some
beer), soup (some soup), rain (some rain), sunshine (some sunshine), breakfast (some breakfast),
dinner (some dinner).

Notice, however, that some can go with both countable and non-countable nouns. Thus we can also
have some boys, some girls, some knives, some pens, etc. where some means a non-definite
number, usually more than two.

Now look at the following:

(a) | There was some sand on the kitchen floor.
(b) | Wehad some rice for supper.

(c) Titi found some bread in the cupboard.

Note that sand is made up of grains which may be too numerous to count. So when we refer to a mass
of sand we regard it as uncountable. However, when we wish to refer to more than one mass, we can
then use the plural sands.

Because we also regard rice as a mass we say some rice. Unlike sand, rice has no plural form at all,
but of course we can say a few grains of rice.

When bread is served at table we regard it as a mass noun, as in (a) and (b) above. However, in order
to make it countable we use the noun loaf and say a loaf of bread, many loaves of bread.
